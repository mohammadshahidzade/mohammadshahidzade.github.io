01 / THE PROBLEM

Assertions are valuable—and expensive to write well.

Formal property verification uses assertions to express the intended behavior of a hardware design. Writing complete, syntactically valid, and functionally correct SystemVerilog Assertions requires both design knowledge and specialist verification experience. Baseline language models often produce plausible-looking properties that fail to compile or do not express the intended behavior.

02 / THE FLOW

Split the reasoning, then close the tool-feedback loop.

Design specificationSubtask-tuned LLMSVA candidateCompiler feedbackRefinement
  • Created a subtask-focused fine-tuning strategy that teaches the model the distinct reasoning steps involved in assertion generation.
  • Built datasets and automation for training, evaluation, compilation, and functional checking.
  • Developed a custom compiler-feedback layer that converts syntax and semantic failures into actionable messages for the model.
  • Added iterative re-prompting so invalid assertions can be repaired rather than discarded.
  • Evaluated generated properties with formal-verification tooling rather than relying only on text similarity.
03 / OUTCOME

Quality improved at both the functional and compiler levels.

Subtask-focused fine-tuning produced a 7.3-fold increase in functionally correct assertions compared with the evaluated baseline. The iterative correction flow increased the number of assertions without syntax errors by 26%.

The work was published at the 26th IEEE International Symposium on Quality Electronic Design in 2025 with Behnam Ghavami, Steven J. E. Wilton, and Lesley Shannon.
